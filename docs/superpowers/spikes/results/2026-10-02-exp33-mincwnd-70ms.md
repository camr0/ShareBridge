# Experiment 33 — `SB_SCTP_MIN_CWND` sweep at ~70 ms (CLIENT-WEST) — 2026-10-02

**Status:** COMPLETE — written incrementally after every cell; 19 cells launched, 16 valid, 3 discarded by the mode gate.

**Verdict: NEGATIVE — E18's closure holds at 70 ms, and the lever is *more* dangerous there, not helpful.**
`SB_SCTP_MIN_CWND` does not become useful when the BDP grows 3.5×. No floor beat the interleaved controls.
**262144 B (≈0.44× BDP) was the only arm whose cells all met or beat the control median, but its +9 % mean is
carried by a single 80.8 Mbps session whose immediately-following control read 67.5 Mbps — session variance, not a
floor benefit.** **393216 B (≈0.66× BDP) already degrades** (mean 51.8 Mbps, −23 %; 2 of 3 cells below the entire
control range), and **524288 / 655360 B (≈0.88–1.10× BDP) collapse reproducibly to ~15 Mbps** (6/6 cells, −77 %,
including cells that never complete at all). **D2 answers the second question decisively: the 70 ms path is a
PLATEAU, not a ramp** — every arm reaches its operating rate within the first few seconds and holds it, so there is
no window-limited slow-start phase for a min-cwnd floor to remove. The degraded arms land on a *hard* 16.2 Mbps
plateau with periodic halvings to 8.2 Mbps (~30–40 s period, an RTO/window-collapse signature) that never recovers
over 210–430 s, while **in-transfer RTT is unchanged (69.7–75.0 ms in every arm)** — the collapse is loss-driven,
not queue-standing.

**Question.** E18 swept small floors (32/64/128/256 KiB) **only on CLIENT-EAST at ~12 ms**, where BDP ≈ 165–186 KiB;
its own results file records that "CLIENT-WEST (71 ms, BDP ≈ 1 MB) not swept". Its closure — "`SB_SCTP_MIN_CWND` has
no field value at any size" — therefore rested on a 12 ms BDP. At ~70 ms the BDP is ≈590 KB, so a 256–640 KiB floor
sits at/below 1× BDP (the "harmless" zone in E18's dose-response), and the open question was whether *harmless*
flips to *helpful* at high RTT. E32 established ~70 ms as a distinct, window-shaped regime (~75 Mbps at only 0.62
renderer cores).

**D2 (secondary):** a 250 ms instantaneous throughput series during the same cells, to distinguish a **ramp**
(slow-start / window-limited → improvable) from a **flat plateau** (hard cap → not improvable).

**Answer to D2: a PLATEAU, not a ramp.** There is no arm — including the controls — in which throughput starts low
and climbs. Slow start at 70 ms RTT spans ~5–6 round trips ≈ **0.4 s**, negligible against a 90 s transfer, and the
control wire rate is already at 80–90 Mbps by t = 5–15 s (F393a's wire buckets: 10 s → 85, 15 s → 96, then 94/81/87/91
… no upward trend). **The residual ~67 Mbps is therefore a hard cap, not a window-limited regime**, and there is no
ramp for a min-cwnd floor to remove. The degraded arms are equally flat: they sit on a **16.2 Mbps** plateau with
periodic halvings to **8.2 Mbps** every ~30–40 s — a sawtooth whose period is far too long for slow start and which
never recovers over 210–430 s — while **in-transfer RTT stays at 69.7–75.0 ms**. Unchanged RTT rules out
queue-standing/bufferbloat; the pattern is the signature of repeated loss → window collapse → RTO recovery, i.e. the
floor's oversized in-flight burst collapsing the window faster than it can grow.

## Interpretation

1. **The lever does not flip to helpful at high RTT.** This was the one untested regime, and the answer is no. At
   256 KiB (0.44× BDP) the floor is inert — the same "harmless" outcome E18 saw at ≤128 KiB on EAST — and the
   apparent +9 % is one session. At 384 KiB the arm is already unreliable, and at 512–640 KiB every cell (6/6)
   collapses to ~15 Mbps, with 4 of 6 never completing inside the driver's window (one completed only at 419.5 s).
   A lever that is sometimes inert and sometimes 4.4× destructive is strictly worse than no lever.
2. **The harm threshold moves up in bytes but *down* as a fraction of BDP.** Safe ceiling: 128 KiB at 12 ms
   (0.70× BDP) → 256 KiB at 70 ms (0.44× BDP). So the safe absolute size roughly doubles while the tolerable BDP
   fraction falls. Neither a constant-byte rule nor a constant-BDP-multiple rule fits: the tolerance sits between
   them. E18's framing ("degradation begins between 0.8× and 1.6× BDP") does not transfer as a ratio — at 70 ms
   degradation begins below 0.9×, *inside* what would have been called the harmless zone. **This is the substantive
   correction to E18:** the harmless zone is not defined by BDP multiples, so a floor chosen to sit "safely below
   1× BDP" on a high-RTT path can still land in the destructive regime.
3. **Why the mechanism matters and remains only partly resolved.** The floor is *supposed* to raise the window, yet
   the degraded arms sit at an effective in-flight volume of ~130–150 KB (15.5 Mbps × 70 ms / 8) — far *below* the
   512 KiB floor. So the connection is not window-limited by the floor at all: the oversized instantaneous burst
   overshoots the bottleneck, and the resulting loss cycles pin throughput at ~15 Mbps. The bottleneck is the same
   VERSA uplink in both E18 and E33, so the difference between the experiments is RTT — but the tolerance does not
   scale cleanly with either RTT or BDP, and these two experiments are not enough to separate "bottleneck buffer
   size" from "SCTP receiver-window / reshuffling limits". Stated as an empirical rule rather than a mechanism:
   **at ~70 ms, floors ≤256 KiB are harmless, 384 KiB is unstable, and ≥512 KiB collapse the transfer.**
4. **The plateau finding explains the null result.** A min-cwnd floor can only help a path whose throughput is
   window-limited by slow start or by loss-triggered window collapse. At 70 ms v1 direct is a flat plateau at its
   operating rate from the first few seconds, and where loss does occur the collapse is *caused* by the
   oversize burst. There is no regime here for the knob to exploit — E14's lab 7–19× wins required synthetic loss
   that the real path does not exhibit at a level a floor can help with.
5. **Practical recommendation: keep `SB_SCTP_MIN_CWND` unset.** Shipping any value ≥384 KiB is actively dangerous
   on 70 ms paths; values ≤256 KiB are inert, and the decision-relevant comparison (262144 vs interleaved controls,
   including the control run immediately after its best cell) shows no benefit. This confirms and strengthens E18.

## Caveats

- **n = 3 per floor, 4 controls**, single session, one client VM, one agent, one ~70 ms path, one 754 MiB payload.
  The rig is demonstrably noisy: the same floor value produced 68.79 and 30.74 Mbps (F393a vs F393bb, ×2.2), and the
  same control produced 64.29–69.58. The 262144 arm's mean is not statistically separable from the controls, and
  neither is the 384 KiB arm's — only the 512/640 KiB collapse (6/6 cells, all in a tight 14.7–16.8 Mbps band, all
  outside the control range) is decisive on its own.
- **The broken arms' numbers are observation windows, not completions.** F524b/c and F655b/c were terminated at
  `MAX_MS=210000` once the trickle was unambiguous; their Mbps are the getStats mean over 210 s and the agent-side
  window does not exist (verbatim `NO-TERMINAL` recorded for each). F524a was allowed the full 360 s and still did
  not complete (88 %); F655a was allowed 600 s and did complete, at 419.5 s. The collapse conclusion rests on
  "0 of 3 completed within 210–360 s at ~15 Mbps" plus the one 419.5 s completion.
- **The client is the unfixed production build** (see the variant note). Numbers are not comparable to E32's *fixed*
  arm (74.71 Mbps); compare to E32's unfixed WEST (64.84) and to E18 (also unfixed).
- **Three cells were discarded by the mode gate** (relay fallback / a join race), all with zero bytes transferred.
  Discard rate ~16 %; if anything this means real relay fallbacks happen at ~70 ms and are only caught because the
  badge is asserted before the click. Any 70 ms field measurement without a direct-mode assertion is untrustworthy.
- **D2's getStats mean is over click → terminal**, so it includes any post-transfer tail (F262cc's 16.0 s tail
  dilutes its getStats mean to 67.94 against an 80.775 agent-side figure). The agent-side window remains the primary
  metric; the series is used for *shape*, not level.
- Agent CPU was sampled opportunistically (30.6–82.4 %), not continuously, and varied within a cell; it is not a
  controlled variable here. The degraded arms ran at *low* agent CPU (3.1–11.2 %), consistent with a stalled sender
  rather than CPU saturation.
- The 16.2 Mbps plateau's micro-mechanism (RTO periodicity) is inferred from the 250 ms series and the constant RTT,
  not from SCTP internal counters.
